What am I looking at?

A lattice animal is a shape made of grid cells stuck together — a polyomino. This is a demo for CIMC's Lattice Animals hackathon (Sep 26, 2026, San Francisco), where each participant brings a mind for a single cell and single cells don't count.

The rules the minds obey

  • Each mind sees only its Voronoi neighbors — no shared clock, no shared name, no message passing.
  • Its only channel is movement. Its only commitment is position.
  • Each mind proposes a local gauge: a spacing (median distance to its neighbors) and a rotation (the dominant 4‑fold orientation of its neighborhood). The global gauge follows the mean of those proposals.
  • Each mind is pulled toward the nearest cell of the current gauge. When it stays there long enough, it commits.
  • Two 4‑adjacent committed minds form a bond. A connected component of 2+ committed minds is a lattice animal.

What you're seeing

  • Colored cells — each mind paints its Voronoi region in its own tint (from the objetd'art palette). Committed cells burn brighter.
  • Vector cilia — a mind's intended next step. Uncommitted minds reach outward; committed minds go still.
  • Warm filaments — bonds between adjacent committed minds. These are the polyomino's actual edges.
  • Cream outline — the perimeter of a lattice animal.
  • Cosmic dust & twinkles — the medium the minds drift through, drawn from the objetd'art stars vibe.

Numbers up top

  • minds — total candidate cells in the field
  • committed — minds currently locked to the shared gauge
  • animals — number of distinct lattice animals right now
  • largest — cells in the biggest current animal
  • entropy — average per‑mind offset from its gauge cell (0 = perfectly still on grid)
