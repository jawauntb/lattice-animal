what's happening in the field

You're watching a hundred small minds try to become a single body without ever talking to each other. Everything they can do is move; everything they can see is where their nearest neighbors are. Over about half a minute they negotiate a shared grid, lock into it one at a time, and connect into small polyominos — those are the lattice animals. After they lock, the story does not stop: they wander, birth, split, and join, and this log keeps speaking. Tap a line to see the place on the field that made it true. Inside each mind, a fly heading circuit loops on the page. A slower thought, 754 neurons on an L4, waits until the field is born, then writes back every few seconds. The page never waits. Once a body exists, that heading bump walks the polyomino as a traveling wave. Two animals add or cancel at the seam.

What am I looking at?

A lattice animal is a shape made of grid cells stuck together — a polyomino. This is a demo for CIMC's Lattice Animals hackathon (Sep 26, 2026, San Francisco), where each participant brings a mind for a single cell and single cells don't count.

The rules the minds obey

  • Each mind sees only its Voronoi neighbors — no shared clock, no shared name, no message passing.
  • Its only channel is movement. Its only commitment is position.
  • Each mind proposes a local gauge: a spacing (median distance to its neighbors) and a rotation (the dominant 4‑fold orientation of its neighborhood). The global gauge follows the mean of those proposals.
  • Each mind is pulled toward the nearest cell of the current gauge. When it stays there long enough, it commits.
  • Two 4‑adjacent committed minds form a bond. A connected component of 2+ committed minds is a lattice animal.
  • Each mind carries a voltage V that drifts toward the mean V of its committed neighbors. Bonds brighten when |ΔV| is small and fade when the drop is large — the same voltage‑gated junction Levin describes in tissue.

What you're seeing

  • Colored cells — each mind paints its Voronoi region in its own tint (from the objetd'art palette). Committed cells burn brighter.
  • Vector cilia — a mind's intended next step. Uncommitted minds reach outward; committed minds go still.
  • Warm filaments — bonds between adjacent committed minds. These are the polyomino's actual edges. Opacity and width follow |ΔV| across the bond: matched voltage is open and bright; a large drop gates the filament shut.
  • Drag the field — a finger or pointer paints a trail of minds and plucks a quiet glass grain. The pitch follows where you are; the loudness follows how fast you move.
  • V ring — a thin teal (negative / hyperpolarized) or coral (positive / depolarized) ring around each mind. Magnitude is how far V sits from zero.
  • Concern blooms — cool teal/blue clouds at spawn, fission, dissolve, merge, and wherever you drop minds. Cells compress toward those peaks; the shared grid spacing itself stays put.
  • Valence threads — seven colored spokes on a searching mind. At commit they fold into one cream line: the vector became a scalar.
  • W‑max fan — a cream family of compatible rotations at the gauge center. Wide while the field is still choosing; it snaps shut only when the family is narrow.
  • Light cones — a cool circle around each searching mind. Overlaps glow brighter. Committed bodies share one fused cone.
  • Remembered holes — dashed cells where a damaged animal still wants to sit someone. The body tries to grow those squares back.
  • Fly circuit — a tiny constellation around a mind is a compiled heading circuit from the Janelia male CNS (EPG, PEN, PEG, Delta7). It loops K times each tick and writes into that cell's voltage. A cream ring means a thought just closed.
  • Traveling wave — the circuit's heading bump walks the animal as a slow beta stencil and a faster gamma report (Miller, Brincat & Roy 2026). Cool pearls ride the gold bonds. At a seam the waves add (one lineage eats another) or cancel (a neck lets go). When one pattern organizes the whole body, that is the higher-order animal.
  • Jev — a System One decision (TypeSafe / Almeida 2026) over the field's own state. Parallel typed answers: should we eat, cut, wander, spawn, or hold; which morphospace inhabitant is this a pointer to. Output tokens are empty on purpose. Code keeps the lattice.
  • Narrator pin — when the verse names an event, a cream mark sits on the field at the place that made it true. Tap a line in the log to see it again.
  • Cream outline — the perimeter of a lattice animal.
  • Cosmic dust & twinkles — the medium the minds drift through, drawn from the objetd'art stars vibe.

Numbers up top

  • minds — total candidate cells in the field
  • committed — minds currently locked to the shared gauge
  • animals — number of distinct lattice animals right now
  • largest — cells in the biggest current animal
  • entropy — average per‑mind offset from its gauge cell (0 = perfectly still on grid)
  • V — mean voltage of committed minds (whole field if none have committed yet)
  • χ — peak of the concern field (1 when quiet; higher near recent events)
  • width — how wide the compatible rotation family still is. Narrow means the gauge has committed; wide means it is still w‑maxing.
  • loop — mean times the fly circuit is reused this tick. Dolphins think deeper; whales think slower.
  • think — opening while the field is being born (no GPU yet); L4 N when a delayed letter from the Modal GPU arrives; local when only the on-page reflex is running.
  • wave — mean beta coherence across animals. Near 1, one traveling pattern holds the body.
  • jev — last typed move from TypeSafe's System One model (wander / spawn / fission / hold), or off when no key is set. Jev never writes a sentence. The field still walks itself.

How to touch it

  • Tap empty field to drop minds. Long‑press a committed cell to take it; the body tries to grow the remembered shape back.
  • The chord / arpeggio button (A) changes only how commits look: together, or smeared across ticks.
  • H, the stacked-lines button next to pause, or ⌇ hides the legend and telemetry. The verse follows the field as it happens.
  • Open the narrator and tap a morphospace shape to bias new births toward that inhabitant.
